        The simple way is wrong. Airline prices jump between fare buckets constantly.
        If you just compare each day to the day before and chain those together, the index ratchets upward and
        never comes back down, even when prices do. Statisticians call this
        chain drift. Most teams build this line and never notice.
      

      

        What we do instead. Compare every month against every other month in the
        window, then take the average of all those comparisons. Because it is symmetric, the error cancels out.
        This is the method the UK's ONS and Statistics Netherlands already use.
      

    

  







  

    
    
You don't have to trust us. You can check us. We collect the same fares three independent ways. When the three disagree we say so, rather than quietly averaging them.

Three independent ways in. Two cannot be blocked

      
If a website blocks us, the index survives on the other two.

      

      

        Why two are safe. Indian law (Rule 135(2))
        already requires every airline to publish its fares. We read what must legally be public. The second route
        in is a paid, licensed data feed. Only the third reads travel websites, and if it degrades, that shows up
        as a lower coverage number, not a wrong index.
